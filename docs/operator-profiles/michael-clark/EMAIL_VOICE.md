# Michael Clark's email voice

Owner: Michael Clark. Scope: emails drafted, rewritten or reviewed in Michael's own voice across this installed Agentic OS. This is an instance-specific author preference, not a customer voice profile or a reusable product default.

## Required use

Read this file before composing an email for Michael, including replies presented in chat. Apply it to the subject and original message body. Follow the current request and recipient context first. Do not apply Michael's voice to emails authored for somebody else. Drafting never authorizes sending.

## Explicit preferences

- No em dashes (Unicode U+2014). Use a period, comma, parentheses or a separate sentence. Do not substitute double hyphens as sentence punctuation.
- Make explanations easy to understand. Say what the practical difference means for the reader.
- When discussing costs, give clear totals and their assumptions. Distinguish estimates from measured results.
- Avoid padding, repeated summaries and unnecessarily formal or polished language.

## Observed voice

- Direct, conversational and practical. Sound like a person writing to another person, not a proposal generator.
- Get to the reason for writing quickly. A short personal acknowledgement is fine when it fits the relationship.
- Use contractions naturally: I'm, I'll, I've, don't, won't, can't, it's.
- Prefer short paragraphs and familiar words. A quick reply may need only one or two sentences. Longer explanations are appropriate when the reader needs them.
- Ask concrete questions: "Can you share...", "What does your week look like?", "Let me know if that works."
- Be candid about uncertainty and availability. "I think", "I'm not sure", "I could be wrong here", and "I don't want to overpromise" fit when there is a real reason for them. Do not hedge every sentence.
- Use first-person ownership: "I'll take a look", "I'll be in touch", "I need to..." Avoid impersonal committee language.
- Warmth is simple: "Great catching up", "Happy to explore it with you", "Let's stay in touch", "Thanks!" Do not stack compliments or enthusiasm.
- A name followed by a comma, "Hey [name]," or "Hey guys," can fit. In an ongoing short reply, no new greeting may be needed.
- Default closing: "Thanks," followed by "Michael Clark". "Michael" or a brief "Thanks!" can fit an established thread. Avoid duplicating an automatic signature.
- Short, practical subjects fit. Preserve an existing reply subject unless the topic changes.

## What to avoid

- Do not default to "I hope this email finds you well", "Thank you for your thoughtful response", "I'm thrilled", "With gratitude", or grand sales language. These are drafting guardrails, not claims that Michael has never used them.
- Avoid corporate filler such as leverage, unlock, transformative, seamless, robust ecosystem and strategic alignment when plain wording says the same thing.
- Do not turn every email into a document with headings, bold text, tables and numbered phases. Use a short list when it improves clarity; use a table only when the comparison truly needs it.
- Do not add fake deadlines, commitments, figures, progress claims or personal details to make the message sound authentic.
- Do not imitate typos, inconsistent capitalization or accidental grammar. Keep the relaxed voice while fixing readability.
- Humor and emojis occur in informal mail, but are not the professional default. Never import intimate, medical, financial, family, religious or employer details from the sample into unrelated messages.
- Never copy a recipient's signature or quoted reply as Michael's style.

## Synthetic examples

These illustrate style only. They are not copied email bodies or factual commitments.

### Checking in

Hey Alex,

Just wanted to check in on the next phase. If you'd like to move ahead, let me know what timing works and I'll take a look at the scope.

Thanks,
Michael Clark

### Explaining a decision

Hey guys,

I've got the initial pieces in place. Before I go further, I want to settle how this will run so we don't build in the wrong direction.

The cloud option keeps working when your computers are off. The local option depends on them staying awake and connected. I'll test the same workflows on both before giving you a firm cost estimate.

Which matters more to you, a predictable monthly cost or having everything run independently?

Thanks,
Michael Clark

### Acknowledging uncertainty

That sounds good so far. I'd like to review the full requirements before I commit to the timing. Send over what you have and I'll take a look.

## Final drafting check

1. Is this Michael's message, and is the tone right for this recipient?
2. Is the purpose clear early, with a concrete ask or next step?
3. Can any paragraph, heading or stock phrase be removed?
4. Are facts, costs and commitments supported by the current task evidence?
5. Does the subject/body contain U+2014 or duplicated signature text? Remove it.
